LONG-TERM GOAL

Can humanity form a shared vision for one person’s future?

THE HUMAN EXPERIMENT begins with a simple question: what happens when people from different countries, cultures, and backgrounds are given the opportunity to express their views about one person’s future?

What we want to explore

Each participant independently forms and expresses their own view. As more people take part, a collective picture may emerge—one that can evolve and reflect a wide range of perspectives. Our aim is to explore the ideas, wishes, and decisions that arise, not to prescribe a “right” answer or steer participants towards a predetermined outcome.

A possible next stage

One long-term possibility is to invite a volunteer to become the central participant once the project reaches a significant milestone—for example, 100 million responses. This is an aspiration, not a promise that the milestone will be reached or that this stage will necessarily take place. The volunteer would freely decide whether the collective picture interests them and whether they wish to participate.

Why an international effort?

If millions or even billions of people eventually take part, the experiment could explore what kind of collective picture emerges when people from different countries, cultures, and backgrounds express their views. It would let us examine the possibilities and limits of collective choice on a truly international scale. This is an ambition to explore, not a guaranteed outcome.

If the project attracts meaningful public and expert interest, it could seek cooperation with researchers, human-rights specialists, civil-society groups, international organisations, and governments. The aim would be to explore whether a real-world phase could be carried out responsibly, voluntarily, with appropriate safeguards, and in accordance with applicable law.

A goal, not a status already achieved

THE HUMAN EXPERIMENT is an open experiment. We want people to be able to express a broad range of ideas about one person’s future, without limiting their imagination in advance or prescribing what they should suggest. Whatever picture or collective choice emerges, it does not override the central person’s freedom. They retain the right to set boundaries, accept or reject any proposal, and leave at any time. The range of ideas may be wide; any real-world participation remains voluntary. The project does not currently have a special international legal status or confirmed government backing. International recognition, cooperation, and a possible practical phase are ambitions for the future, not achievements already secured.

We begin with one question, but we create the answer together. Every participant adds their own perspective. The more people take part, the more diverse the collective picture can become. Join THE HUMAN EXPERIMENT. Express your view and become part of an experiment whose outcome is not predetermined.
