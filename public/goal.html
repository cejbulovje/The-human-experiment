AN INTERNATIONAL THOUGHT EXPERIMENT

Who would one person become if each of us could change them?

Suppose the world agrees on one thing. This person should be happy. Tomorrow morning, everyone has to decide what that means.

Some would fulfil that person’s deepest wish. Someone else might change what makes them feel happy in the first place. Would each agree to live with what the other proposed?

The two roles

You can explore two roles. In the main survey, imagine helping decide what happens to the volunteer. In the personal preferences preview, imagine being that volunteer and consider what you would entrust to others.

Why invite people from around the world?

We want to learn what these choices reveal about humanity: our desires, values, contradictions, and how we think together about freedom and trust.

What would happen if the volunteer’s wishes conflicted with those of the majority? Could the experiment help us understand what people truly want, where their values clash, and what those tensions reveal about human nature? The aim is to learn about humanity; the prospect of changing one person gives us a way to ask those questions.

Today and in the future

Today the main survey collects independent opinions about what each person would choose. The personal preferences preview lets you reflect on your wishes and motives. There is no process for making binding collective decisions.

A future line of research could explore how people weigh conflicting proposals, find common ground and make a collective choice. Any practical stage would be a separate undertaking.

Rights and consent

The transfer of power exists only in the imagined scenario. The person keeps all their rights. Any practical stage would require separate informed consent, and the person could refuse any proposal or withdraw at any time.

There is no morally correct answer set in advance. You can propose a change, leave the decision to the person, or choose to leave their life entirely as it is.
