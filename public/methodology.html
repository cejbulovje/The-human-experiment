RESEARCH APPROACH

Research methodology

THE HUMAN EXPERIMENT explores how people express wishes, values, and views about one person’s future, and what patterns may emerge when independent responses are considered together.

1. Core principle

Each participant is invited to provide an independent response. The project aims to observe the range of views and identify agreement and disagreement, not present one person’s opinion as everyone’s opinion.

2. Two distinct response streams

The project separates two questions that should not be mixed: the collective perspective on a hypothetical person’s future, and personal preferences about one’s own life in that situation.

3. Interpreting results

Responses may be grouped by recurring themes and compared. Counts and percentages should include sample sizes and explain how responses were selected. Results describe the people who responded; they do not automatically represent all humanity or prove what is objectively best for anyone.

4. Current limitations

This website is still a prototype. The hypothetical-preferences page does not currently collect or store submitted answers. The experiment author’s example is not an independent participant response and must not be treated as research data.

5. Autonomy, consent, and privacy

This is a thought experiment, not an actual process for transferring control over anyone’s life. Participation should be voluntary. Any future data collection or analysis must follow the site’s privacy information and clearly explain how responses are handled.

6. Next steps

The next steps are to refine the questions, implement and test a clear collection process, explain how responses are handled, and publish results only when enough valid data exists to report them responsibly.

This page describes the intended approach. It does not claim that every proposed collection, comparison, or analysis feature is already implemented.
