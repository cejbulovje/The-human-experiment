RESEARCH APPROACH

What can an answer to an impossible question tell us?

The experiment assumes that one person has voluntarily given humanity complete power over their fate and that anything people agree on can happen. The first question lets each person propose whatever they can imagine.

1. Core principle

Each participant answers independently, starting with their own proposal in their own words. They can also leave everything as it is or question the premise.

2. Two distinct response streams

You can explore two roles. In the main survey, imagine helping decide what happens to the volunteer. In the personal preferences preview, imagine being that volunteer and consider what you would entrust to others.

3. Interpreting results

“I would do this,” “I think most people would do this,” and “I would agree to this happening to me” answer three different questions. The results describe the people who chose to respond.

4. Current limitations

The published totals include the project author’s example. It is identified separately and is not an independent participant response. The personal preferences form is a preview and does not collect new answers.

5. Autonomy, consent, and privacy

The transfer of power exists only in the imagined scenario. The person keeps all their rights. Any practical stage would require separate informed consent, and the person could refuse any proposal or withdraw at any time.

6. Next steps

Today the main survey collects independent opinions about what each person would choose. The personal preferences preview lets you reflect on your wishes and motives. There is no process for making binding collective decisions. A future line of research could explore how people weigh conflicting proposals, find common ground and make a collective choice. Any practical stage would be a separate undertaking.

This page describes the intended approach. It does not claim that every proposed collection, comparison, or analysis feature is already implemented.
